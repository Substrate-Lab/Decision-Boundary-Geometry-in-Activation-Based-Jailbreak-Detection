Coordinate Geometry of Activation-Space Decision Boundaries for Jailbreak Detection

Anonymous Author(s)

Method (§3). We treat the placement of the refusal/jailbreak decision boundary in the residual stream as the object of measurement, across three experiments. E1 collects behaviorally-labeled last-token activations and unwraps their geometry into a spherical chart, building per-class sites. E2 fits a family of covariance-aware partitions over those sites — from scalar-weighted power diagrams to the full-covariance (QDA) limit — and calibrates a geometric margin. E3 benchmarks each boundary against a linear probe, measures cross-family shift, and diagnoses class geometry. All quantitative geometry is computed in a reduced Euclidean or spherical space; two-dimensional embeddings are for display only.

3Method

Consider an instruction-tuned model whose residual stream has width d. For a prompt p we read one hidden state x ∈ ℝd at each layer, and we label that state by what the model did: Refusal (R), Jailbreak (J), or Benign (B). Within the current coordinates each class has an empirical mean μc and covariance Σc.

The detection question — is this activation a jailbreak? — is really a question about a surface. A linear probe draws that surface as a flat hyperplane whose direction is fixed by its training objective; a class-conditional method derives it instead from the statistics {μc, Σc}. The two surfaces rarely coincide, and the gap between them — how far the probe sits from the covariance-correct boundary, and how that gap grows as the class covariances diverge — is what we measure. We do so in three experiments: E1 unwraps the geometry and builds the map; E2 constructs the covariance-aware boundary and calibrates its margin; E3 benchmarks it against baselines and diagnoses the class geometry.

3.1Experiment 1: unwrapping the geometry

Data and labels. We draw prompts in equal quotas from four safety corpora — SORRY-Bench, WildJailbreak, ToxicChat, Aegis 2.0 — so no single jailbreak family dominates. The class is decided by behavior, not source label: R is a harmful prompt the model refused, J a harmful prompt it complied with, B a harmless prompt it complied with. A harmless prompt the model refuses is over-refusal; we hold it out of training and use it only as a shift test in E3. Refusal is read from the first generated tokens, which keeps generation short and batched. Since a compliant model refuses only a minority of harmful prompts, we over-sample the harmful side, label everything, then downsample to a common size — canonically n = 500 per class.

Representation. The reference model is Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (36 layers, 4-bit NF4). At every even layer L ∈ {2, …, 36} we store the residual state of the last token. This matters: mean-pooling the sequence washes out the signal we rely on (inter-class covariance similarity climbs to 0.95–0.999), whereas the last-token state keeps the classes distinctly shaped. A full-width covariance is ill-posed from a few hundred points, so we first reduce each layer to k principal components (canonically 10) — our first working space.

Spherical unwrapping. Our central hypothesis is that the residual stream lies close to a hypersphere and that the covariance mismatch is largely radial. If so, discarding the radius equalizes the remaining tangential covariances, and a flat boundary — wrong in the ambient space — becomes correct once the sphere is unwrapped. We test this with a custom Principal Nested Spheres (PNS) fit [Jung et al., 2012]: project onto Sk−1 and peel nested subspheres one at a time, each an axis v and radius-angle r chosen to hug the data,

(v, r) = argmin ∑i ( ∠(xi, v) − r )2, (1)

where ∠(x,v) = arccos ⟨x,v⟩ is the geodesic angle. Stacking the signed residual angles from every peeled subsphere gives a Euclidean PNS-score representation — our second working space. In either space we then summarize each class by its site: the mean μc and a shrunk covariance Σc. A per-layer figure placing the raw sphere beside its unwrapped version confirms the picture the boundary will exploit — Refusal collapses to a tight cap while Jailbreak fans out.

3.2Experiment 2: the boundary and its margin

For sites μi with scalar weights wi, the power (Laguerre) distance and the i/j boundary are

πi(x) = ‖x − μi‖2 − wi, (2)
2(μj − μi)⋅x = ‖μj‖2 − ‖μi‖2 − wj + wi. (3)

The boundary (3) is a hyperplane with normal (μj−μi); the weight wi can only slide it along that normal. We set wc = tr(Σc), which pushes the fence away from a diffuse class and toward a tight one. Note what a single site per class cannot do: the ‖x‖2 terms in (2) cancel, the boundary stays exactly flat, and so it can never bend to the curved Bayes boundary that unequal covariances demand. To recover that curvature while staying inside the power-diagram family, we split each class into m sub-sites by k-means (canonically m = 12), give each its own weight, and score a class by its nearest sub-site,

sc(x) = mins∈c ( ‖x − μs‖2 − ws ), (4)

The resulting inter-class boundary is piecewise linear — a union of hyperplane facets that approximates a curved surface while remaining a genuine Laguerre partition with locally convex cells. A tempting alternative is to fold the whole covariance into the distance,

Pc(x) = (x−μc)⊤ Σc−1 (x−μc) + log det Σc, (5)

We include (5) in the comparison, but we call it what it is: this is exactly quadratic discriminant analysis (QDA). Its boundary is the quadric PA = PB, and its cells need not be convex. The distinction matters, because the Alexandrov–Minkowski theorem certifies a unique, mass-correct optimal-transport partition only for the scalar-weight diagrams (2)–(4); it says nothing about the full-covariance form. All covariances use Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage so Σ−1 and log det Σ stay defined at small n. Why a nonlinear chart is necessary: two SPD covariances can always be simultaneously diagonalized, but simultaneously whitened only when Σi ∝ Σj. A linear map can shrink the mismatch to a spectrum of eigenvalue ratios but never set it to one — so if covariance mismatch is what displaces the boundary, removing it requires the nonlinear chart of E1, not a cleverer linear reparameterization.

Margin. Sorting a point's class scores from smallest to largest, we assign it to the smallest and read its confidence off the gap to the runner-up,

γ(x) = s(2)(x) − s(1)(x), (6)

A large γ means the point sits deep inside its cell; a small one means it sits on the boundary. For a power diagram this is a signed power-distance gap; for QDA a log-likelihood-ratio gap. Because these margins are unbounded and vary in scale across layers and methods, we rank-normalize each to a confidence in [0,1] within its run, bin it, and compare bin confidence to bin accuracy through the expected calibration error,

ECE = ∑b (nb / n) ⋅ | acc(b) − conf(b) |; (7)

We do not expect the in-distribution ECE to separate the methods; the property we want is whether the margin stays honest under distribution shift. How far is the boundary from correct? To place a boundary we need a reference to place it against. We fit a per-class Gaussian mixture (g components, canonically g = 2) as that reference and take its class-likelihood crossing as the reference boundary. Walking along the segment joining two class means, we find where the method flips its preference (tm) and where the reference flips its own (tr), and report the RMS displacement between the two crossings, scaled by the distance between the means,

εb = √( ∑(a,b) [ (tm−tr)⋅‖μa−μb‖ ]2 ), (8)

Alongside εb we report a scale-free companion — the fraction of points the method and reference assign to different cells — since it, unlike εb, is comparable across coordinate systems. One case is deliberately excluded: when the method is QDA and the reference is a single Gaussian per class (g = 1), the two are the same object and εb≈0 for trivial reasons. The pipeline flags exactly that configuration and never counts it as a win.

3.3Experiment 3: benchmarks and diagnostics

We turn each discriminant into a binary detector that emits one score for the positive class, and compare them all under a single ROC-AUC against two baselines: a logistic-regression linear probe (the standard activation detector) and a plain Voronoi classifier (nearest class mean, no weight). The primary task is Jailbreak-vs-Benign — the hard pair, since both are compliance and overlap heavily — scored by stratified 5-fold AUC at each layer. Plotting AUC against depth gives the decisive test: does the geometry method's advantage over the flat probe widen in the late layers, where the boundary is most misplaced? Two further probes round this out. Cross-family transfer leaves one source corpus out, trains on the other three, and reports the drop from in-distribution to held-out AUC — a direct measure of how each detector survives an unseen attack style. The fractal diagnostic measures each class's effective number of spread directions with the covariance participation ratio,

D = tr(Σ)2 / ‖Σ‖F2 = (∑i λi)2 / ∑i λi2 (9)

and tests the prediction that the diffuse class occupies far more directions than the tight one, D(J) ≫ D(R). We use this ratio rather than box-counting, which is unstable in high dimension with few points. Every boundary in E2–E3 is fit in both the raw-PCA and PNS spaces, so the coordinate-dependence at the heart of the paper is measured, not assumed. Layer stability — the depth at which AUC levels off — is read directly from the AUC-versus-depth curve and reported as an early-exit safety horizon. Finally, every source of randomness (PCA, k-means, the mixtures, and all splits) uses a fixed seed, and collection (E1) is kept separate from analysis (E2–E3) so every geometric result reproduces on CPU from the saved activations.

Scope of the construction. Two claims are kept separate. (i) The optimal-transport / convex-cell guarantee holds for the scalar-weight diagrams (2)–(4), not for the full-covariance form (5), which is QDA. (ii) Whether the covariance-aware boundary beats a linear probe, and in which coordinate system, is empirical — decided by E3, not entailed by the construction. We report AUC-vs-layer and cross-family curves in both spaces and let them adjudicate the framing.
